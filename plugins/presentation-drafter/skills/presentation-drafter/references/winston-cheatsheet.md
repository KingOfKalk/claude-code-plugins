# Patrick Winston's "How to Speak" — Quick Reference

> Core formula: Quality = Knowledge + Practice (talent is negligible).
> Source: MIT IAP Lecture, delivered annually 1978–2019.

## Opening

- Start with an **empowerment promise**: tell people what they'll gain.
  _"By the end you will know X that you don't know now."_
- Do NOT start with a joke (audience isn't warmed up).
- For persuasive talks: **5 minutes** to show your vision + prove you've done something.

## Four Engagement Heuristics

| Technique              | What it does                          | How to apply                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cycling**            | Compensates for ~20% audience fog-out | Cover each key idea **3×** from different angles                             |
| **Building a Fence**   | Prevents confusion with similar ideas | State what it **IS** and what it **IS NOT**                                  |
| **Verbal Punctuation** | Re-entry points for drifted listeners | Enumerate and signpost: _"First… Second… We've now finished X, moving to Y"_ |
| **Asking Questions**   | Jolts audience into active mode       | Calibrate difficulty, then **wait 7 full seconds**                           |

## Time, Place & Setup

- **Best time:** 11:00 AM
- **Lights:** Always fully on
- **Room:** Must be >50% full
- **Devices:** Ban laptops & phones (humans have one language processor)

## Tools: Board vs. Slides vs. Props

| Tool           | Best for                   | Why                                                         |
| -------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Blackboard** | Teaching                   | Matches cognitive speed; hands have purpose; mirror neurons |
| **Slides**     | Exposing ideas at scale    | Visual impact — but only if done right                      |
| **Props**      | Making ideas unforgettable | Physical objects stick in memory for decades                |

### Slide Rules

- No reading slides aloud
- No bullet points
- No logos, backgrounds, slide titles (say the title)
- Font ≥ 40pt
- No laser pointers — use embedded numbered arrows
- Stand near the screen
- Print test: mostly images + white space
- One complex slide max per talk (hapax legomenon)
- **Every word on your slide competes with and defeats every word from your mouth.**

## How to Inspire

1. Express genuine passion about the subject
2. Show belief in the audience's ability
3. Help people see a familiar problem in a new way

## The Winston Star ⭐ — Making Ideas Stick

| Element          | Question                                                  |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Symbol**       | Is there a visual icon for my work?                       |
| **Slogan**       | Can someone describe my idea in one phrase?               |
| **Surprise**     | What defies expectations?                                 |
| **Salient Idea** | What single concept sticks out?                           |
| **Story**        | Can I narrate how I did it, how it works, why it matters? |

## VSN-C Structure (Persuasive Talks)

For job talks, pitches, conference presentations:

1. **Vision** — Problem someone cares about + your new approach
2. **Steps** — What's needed to solve it
3. **News** — Your recent results
4. **Contributions** — What YOU specifically did (mirrors Steps)

Practice with people who **don't** know your work.

## How to End

**Bad final slides:** "Questions?" / "The End" / "Thank You" / collaborator list / URL

**Correct final slide:** **"Contributions"** — list what you accomplished. Stays up during Q&A.

**Final words — pick one:**
- A joke (audience is now calibrated to your humor)
- A call to action / benediction
- Salute the audience

**Never say "thank you" to end.** Only mouth silent thanks after spontaneous applause.
